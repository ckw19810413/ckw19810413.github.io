## Technique 1: Persona Setup (With Full Prompt Example)

This is the simplest yet most powerful technique. When you assign a specific role (Persona) to the AI, it automatically adjusts its tone, depth of knowledge, and response structure.

### ❌ A Bad Prompt

> Tell me how to do social media marketing.

The answer you'll typically get is broad and shallow: "Post content, engage with followers, analyze data…" Well, yes, but that's not really helpful.

### ✅ Using Persona Setup

> You are a social media marketing director with 10 years of experience who has worked with over 50 brands. You excel at explaining strategy through real-world case studies. Please create a 30-day plan for me to help a "startup tech team build an Instagram account from scratch." Requirements:
> 1. One theme per week
> 2. Suggested post type for each day
> 3. One measurable goal per week
> 4. Writing examples for each post type

### Why This Works

Assigning a persona triggers the AI to draw on expert-level knowledge in that domain. Instead of giving you generic "social media marketing principles," it produces a structured, actionable 30-day plan — that's the massive difference persona setup makes.

**Universal Formula: You are [Role] + You have [Experience/Credentials] + Please [Specific Task] + Requirements [Format/Structure]**

## Technique 2: Step-by-Step Guidance

Complex human tasks are possible because we break big goals into small steps. You should do the same when asking AI for help.

### Real-World Example

Let's say you want the AI to write a product launch copy, but you don't want to give it all instructions at once. Here's how you can approach it:

> **Step 1**: You are an experienced copywriter. Please write a target audience analysis for my "AI Prompt Engineering Course." The product is priced at $2,999, and the target audience is working professionals aged 25–45. List 5 typical audience profiles.

Once the AI responds, follow up with:

> **Step 2**: Based on the audience profiles just analyzed, write the opening paragraph of a Landing Page targeting "Audience #3: a mid-career manager who wants to improve work efficiency within 3 months." Address their pain point: spending too many hours on repetitive tasks with no time to learn new skills.

This "step-by-step" approach is like collaborating with a true expert — you guide them step by step toward the result you want. Compared to a vague command like "write a complete marketing copy," the quality of output at each step is exponentially better.

## Technique 3: Chain of Thought

"Chain of Thought" asks the AI to show its reasoning process before giving you an answer. This technique is especially powerful for tasks requiring precise analysis, mathematical computation, or complex decision-making.

### How to Use It

Add this line to the end of any prompt:

> Before answering, please think through your reasoning step by step and show your thought process.

### Real-World Example

Let's say you want the AI to help you do a market analysis:

> I need to decide whether to launch an "AI Prompt Toolkit" product. There are currently three major competitors in the market, with price ranges between $199 and $599.
>
> Please think through step by step:
> 1. First, analyze the overall demand trends in this market
> 2. List 3 potential differentiation advantages for us
> 3. Evaluate pricing strategy, considering competitor prices and target customers' willingness to pay
> 4. Finally, provide a comprehensive recommendation: is it worth doing? If yes, what price do you suggest?

If you just asked for a conclusion, the AI might say "Yes, it's worth it — I recommend pricing at $299." You'd have no idea why. But if you require it to show its thinking process, you can evaluate whether its analysis is reliable, spot any gaps, and then make adjustments.

**Important**: In actual prompts, avoid vague phrases like "think step by step." Instead, be specific: "First list all relevant factors, then analyze each one individually, and finally provide a comprehensive evaluation." The more specific, the better.

## Technique 4: Few-Shot Examples

Few-shot learning means giving the AI a few "input → output" examples so it learns your preferred style or format. This is particularly effective for tasks that require consistent quality.

### Real-World Example

You want the AI to write product reviews, but you don't want the format to vary each time:

> Please write product reviews for me. The examples below show the format you should follow:
>
> **Example 1**:
> Input: Product Name: AI Prompt Library; Price: $29; Key Features: 200+ prompt templates
> Output: 📊 Overall Rating: 4.5/5
> 💡 Highlights: The prompt quality exceeded expectations and covers very comprehensive scenarios, from copywriting to programming.
> ⚠️ Shortcomings: It would be perfect if there were more advanced scenario prompts.
> 🎯 Best For: Beginner to advanced users who need to learn prompt engineering systematically.
>
> **Example 2**:
> Input: Product Name: Feishu Operations Template; Price: $49; Key Features: Project management, OKR, CRM templates
> Output: 📊 Overall Rating: 4.8/5
> 💡 Highlights: The templates cover nearly all scenarios for SME daily operations.
> ⚠️ Shortcomings: Some advanced features require a learning curve.
> 🎯 Best For: Small teams undergoing digital transformation.
>
> **Now please write**:
> Input: Product Name: AI Teaching Course; Price: $99; Key Features: A complete course to learn prompt engineering from scratch

See? You don't need many examples — usually 2–3 is enough. The AI will pick up the format, tone, and level of detail you want.

## Technique 5: Self-Correction

This is my favorite technique — not because it's the most advanced, but because it gets the AI to "check its own mistakes."

### How to Use It

After giving the AI a task, add this line:

> Now please review your previous answer. Are there any errors, incomplete sections, or areas that could be improved? If so, please provide a revised version.

### Real-World Example

You ask the AI to write a product description:

> Write a product description under 150 words introducing our AI Prompt Library.

After the AI responds, follow up with:

> Great, thank you. Now put on the hat of a critical product manager and please:
> 1. Identify the three weakest points of this description
> 2. Point out any customer benefits that aren't communicated clearly
> 3. Provide an improved version

This "play the critic then produce a better version" approach typically lifts the output quality by over 50%. On the first pass, the AI doesn't have time for self-reflection — but give it that opportunity, and it will deliver.
